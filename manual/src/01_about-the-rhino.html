About the Rhino

One motion system, five built-in tools, and room for more: the Rhino changes discipline by changing the tool on its X-carriage, and Klipper changes behaviour to match.

FeatureRhino
MotionCoreXY, 15 mm GT2 belts on X and Y (3 m per belt, 6 m total)
Axis travel (from printer.cfg)X 576 mm, Y 406 mm, Z 400 mm
X axisMGW7 linear rail with MGW7H carriage on an aluminium-tube gantry
Z axisThree leadscrews (rear T12×2, front T8×2) on kinematic couplings; automatic tramming with Z_TILT_ADJUST
Build plateAluminium heat spreader with silicone heater mats, air-gap insulated; designed as an all-purpose vacuum bed
Enclosure3 mm ACP panels with twin front doors
Tool changeTool slides onto the carriage's V-groove, held by one M3×50 bolt and knurled nut; one 21-pin umbilical plug
ToolsPrint heads (BlockOne, SwitchFly), LightSaber laser, HotJoe CNC spindle, DragKnife, plus tools you add
Air“Fresh Air” lines: print part cooling, laser blow-air, spindle chip evacuation
ControllerBTT Octopus (STM32F446)
SoftwareKlipper, Moonraker, Mainsail, the Rhino Tool Manager; OrcaSlicer for printing, LaserWeb 4 for laser and drag knife (Kiri:Moto is being looked at)

The design files, drawings and build notes are in the Makersmic Rhino-3d-Printer repository on GitHub (see Build reference). Still in development there: the “Cheeky Monkey” filament box (filament monitoring, air handling, chip exhaust) and a needle-cutter tool.
